## PANEL 6C — STATUS: DIGITIZATION-DIVERGENT (critical)

**The candidate misses the figure's main effect — the flank crossing.** The paper
*does* show attend_fixation rising above attend_contralateral on the flanks (the
sharpening signature). The digitizer concluded "no crossing in the scan" and refused
to fabricate one. That conclusion is **wrong**: the crossing is present and
recoverable from the pixels. The digitizer drew two non-crossing curves by assigning
"contralateral = upper pixel line" across the *whole* range, which is correct only
near the peak and inverted on the flanks beyond the crossing.

### What is faithful (confirmed independently)

- **Peak enhancement ≈ 0.10 — CONFIRMED.** Two resolvable apexes: enhanced at
  pixel-row 8 (val 1.000), fixation at row 21 (val 0.903), diff **0.097**. Matches
  the candidate's 1.00 vs 0.903.
- **Calibration acceptable.** `detect_plot_box → (11,6,258,143)`. Digitizer
  x-anchors (px54→−180, px258→180) put the apex at −1.8° (≈0, within 1 px) — symmetric
  bell, fine. y-anchors (row142→0.0 baseline, row8→1.0 = larger-peak normalization)
  match the paper's "Normalized Response 0–1". No mis-scaled axis.
- **Two same-color bell curves, centered ≈0°, linear x — correct frame and count.**

### The divergence — flank crossing (the crux)

Using ONLY the zone immediately adjacent to the peak (right flank cols 158–174,
deg 3.5–32), where curve identity is **unambiguous** (each line continuous from its
own apex, no merge), I traced both curves:

| deg | enhanced (top, from row-8 apex) | fixation (bot, from row-21 apex) | gap (ctr−fix) |
|----:|----:|----:|----:|
| 3.5  | 0.993 | 0.903 | **+0.090** |
| 14.1 | 0.959 | 0.873 | +0.086 |
| 22.9 | 0.903 | 0.836 | +0.067 |
| 28.2 | 0.858 | 0.810 | +0.049 |
| 31.8 | 0.828 | 0.791 | **+0.037** |

The peak gap is **+0.090**, and it **shrinks monotonically** toward 0 as |dir| grows.
Fitted descent rates over this unambiguous zone:
**enhanced = 0.0060 val/deg vs fixation = 0.0040 val/deg** — the enhanced
(contralateral) curve falls **1.48× faster**, i.e. it is **NARROWER**. A taller-but-
narrower curve *must* cross below the lower-but-wider one on the outer flank. Two
clean independent Gaussian fits to the upper/lower envelope confirm this:
enhanced amp 0.991 / σ 53.8° (FWHM 127°), fixation amp 0.908 / σ 57.8° (FWHM 136°);
**they cross at |dir| ≈ 61°.** Beyond ~60°, the WIDER fixation curve is the higher one.

This is exactly the R&H sharpening signature the task describes (higher peak AND
narrower → flank crossing). The crossing falls inside the two same-color lines'
mid-flank *merge zone* (cols ~176–189 / |dir| ~48–60°), where the ~2 px lines
coincide for ~13 columns — which is *why* the digitizer, looking only at where the
lines re-separate, mis-read it.

### Why the candidate's "no-crossing" reading is the mistake (not a tie)

The digitizer's own JSON assigns, on the right flank, `contralateral = the upper
pixel line` at every deg (e.g. deg 70: ctr 0.472 = top line 0.46–0.48; fix 0.429 =
bottom line). My independent overlay (`/tmp/my_overlay_6C.png`) reproduces
`overlay_6C.png` exactly: red(contralateral) sits above green(fixation) **everywhere,
never crossing.** But the curve that is on top at the peak descends *faster*, so on
the flank it is the **bottom** line, not the top. The digitizer kept the label glued
to the upper envelope through the merge and thereby **swapped the curve identity past
the crossing**. The result is two non-crossing curves with a fabricated-looking
constant ~0.03–0.04 flank gap — which is itself a tell: the *real* near-peak gap is
+0.09 and shrinking, not a constant 0.03–0.04.

Correct reading: beyond |dir| ≈ 60°, **attend_fixation is the upper (higher) line and
attend_contralateral the lower line.** The digitized `attend_contralateral` flank
points (e.g. 70°→0.472, 85°→0.343, 100°→0.234, 120°→0.130) and `attend_fixation`
points (70°→0.429, 85°→0.298, 100°→0.194, 120°→0.099) are **transposed** past the
crossing.

### Severity

**Critical.** The flank crossing is the *whole point* of feature-based-attention
sharpening (Fig 6C). A reference that renders the two curves as strictly non-crossing
(contralateral above fixation at all directions) is a ruler that will *pass* a model
which only scales the peak and *fail* a model that correctly sharpens (narrows) the
contralateral tuning — i.e. it inverts the test's discriminating power on the figure's
central claim.

### Process note

Calibration + an overlay validation step are present (good). But the curve-tracing
that mattered — separating two same-color, mid-flank-merging lines — was effectively
eyeballed as "upper line = contralateral throughout." The available
`trace_darkest_in_band` returns a single darkest path and cannot, by itself, resolve
two coincident lines or carry identity through a merge; a per-column dark-pixel
*cluster* trace plus a continuity/slope argument from the peak (as done here) is
required and was not applied. Not a tool-absence BLOCKER (the tools suffice, as
demonstrated), so this is filed under the DIGITIZATION-DIVERGENT finding rather than
TOOL-MISUSE, but the method gap is the proximate cause.

## Refute pass (skeptical self-check — findings that survive)

1. *Mis-clustered near-peak lines?* No — 17 consecutive columns, clean 2-px-separated
   clusters, gap shrinking smoothly +0.090→+0.037. Robust.
2. *Faster enhanced descent = noise?* No — monotone over 17 points; rate 0.0060 vs
   0.0040 is a real trend, not jitter.
3. *Could they be equal-width, non-crossing (digitizer's reading)?* Impossible: equal
   width cannot make the taller curve descend faster. Measured faster descent of the
   taller curve = narrower width = guaranteed crossing.
4. *JPEG artifact / am I imagining the second line?* No — two distinct apexes are
   visible in the raw 8× peak crop, two lines on both flank crops; and R&H Fig 6C is a
   published sharpening (narrowing) result. Consistent.
5. *Is this a SUSPECTED-PAPER-ISSUE (paper itself doesn't show it)?* No — the paper
   pixels show it; the divergence is in the digitization, not the paper.

All findings survive. **Verdict: the paper shows the flank crossing; the candidate
does not — DIGITIZATION-DIVERGENT (critical), missing the sharpening crossing.** Do
not treat 6C's digitized reference as binding until the flank assignment is corrected
(curves transposed beyond |dir| ≈ 60°, peak gap +0.10 narrowing to a crossing near
±60°).
