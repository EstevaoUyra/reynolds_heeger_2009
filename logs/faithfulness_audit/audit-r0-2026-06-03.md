## Step 3 — Figures vs paper images (binding dimensions)

### DIVERGENT (implementation) — CRFs do not saturate [Figs 2A, 2B, 3C, 3F, 4E]
Paper Figs 2/3/4 model panels show CRFs that **saturate** to a plateau at high
contrast (sigmoids that bend over); the paper states it explicitly: "Resulting
neural responses saturate at high stimulus contrasts due to normalization … When
c≫σ, r(c)≈α." With σ=0.1 the closed form half-saturates at c≈0.1, so by c=1 the
response should be ~0.9α and visibly flat.

The freshly-rendered curves are still **rising steeply at c=1** — only the
rising flank, no plateau. Numerically (run_figure_2A/2B, n=8): the final
log-contrast segment alone contributes 13% (2A) / 17% (2B) of the endpoint
value; the curve has not leveled. Same shape in figure_3.png (3C/3F) and
figure_4.png (4E rising to ~7 unbounded). This is the 1D over-broad suppressive
pool (SQ-001, acknowledged in docstrings) only partially compensated by the
sigma-scale/gain knobs. Binding "curve shape" dimension diverges from the paper.
Note: `test_figure_2A_crfs_are_monotonic_and_saturating` passes on a *lenient*
bound ((c=1 vs c=0.5)/c=0.5 < 0.35), so the green test does not catch the
visual non-saturation — the test agrees in direction but is too loose to be the
saturation referent. Severity: major. Locus: `protocols._run_figure_2_panel`,
`implementation/calibration.yaml` regime/figure suppressive knobs;
`figure_outputs/figure_{2,3,4}.png`.
