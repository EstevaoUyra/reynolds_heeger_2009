# Figure 6 - Visual Checklist

Each item is a binary pass/fail visual claim. No paper access is required; all context
needed to evaluate the generated figure is included here. Items tagged `<!-- UNSURE -->`
depend on a subtle visual relationship and deserve extra attention during review.

---

## Coordinate convention

The reproducible model output for Figure 6 is panel C: a motion-direction tuning curve
generated by the `figure_6C` protocol. Panels A and B are paper-context panels: panel A is
the experimental task schematic and panel B is empirical data adapted from Martinez-Trujillo
and Treue (2004). They are not generated model outputs unless an implementation explicitly
chooses to include coarse context placeholders.

- **Panel C horizontal axis:** stimulus motion direction, swept from -180 degrees at the
  left edge through the recorded neuron's preferred direction at 0 degrees in the center
  to +180 degrees at the right edge. A generated figure may use numeric tick labels,
  direction arrows, or both.
- **Panel C vertical axis:** normalized response of the model neuron recorded at receptive
  field center x = 0 and preferred direction theta = 0.
- **Curve encoding:** one curve is "attend fixation" with a direction-flat attention field;
  the other curve is "attend stimulus contralateral to receptive field" with spatial
  attention away from the RF and feature-based attention matched to the current stimulus
  direction. A generated figure may use color, line style, or a legend, but the two
  conditions must be visually distinguishable and labeled.
- **Sharpening:** the contralateral-stimulus-attention curve is sharper if it has a smaller
  full width at half maximum than the attend-fixation curve.

## Panel A - Experimental schematic context

Panel A is an empirical task schematic and is not the generated model simulation target.
If an implementation includes a coarse context schematic, only the following high-level
parameter encodings should be checked.

- [ ] Any included schematic shows one stimulus in the recorded receptive field and a second
      stimulus in the opposite hemifield.
- [ ] Any included schematic marks spatial attention away from the recorded receptive field.
- [ ] Any included schematic distinguishes the fixation-attention condition from the
      opposite-hemifield-stimulus attention condition.

## Panel C - Model simulation: feature-based attention sharpens tuning

- [ ] The panel contains exactly two tuning curves plotted against stimulus motion direction.
- [ ] The vertical axis is labeled as normalized response, or an equivalent normalized model
      response quantity.
- [ ] The horizontal direction convention places the preferred direction at the center of
      the plot.
- [ ] Both curves have a single central peak at or very near the preferred direction.
- [ ] Neither curve has secondary side peaks away from the preferred direction.
- [ ] Both curves decline from the central preferred-direction peak toward the left side of
      the plot.
- [ ] Both curves decline from the central preferred-direction peak toward the right side of
      the plot.
- [ ] The attend-fixation curve is visibly broader around the central peak than the
      contralateral-stimulus-attention curve.
- [ ] The contralateral-stimulus-attention curve has a smaller full width at half maximum
      than the attend-fixation curve.
- [ ] The two curves are horizontally aligned: feature-based attention sharpens the tuning
      without shifting the preferred direction left or right.
- [ ] Near the central preferred direction, the contralateral-stimulus-attention curve is at
      least as high as the attend-fixation curve. <!-- UNSURE: the peak-height ordering can be visually subtle if the curves nearly overlap at the center -->
- [ ] Away from the preferred direction, in both shoulders of the tuning curve, the
      contralateral-stimulus-attention curve falls below the attend-fixation curve.
- [ ] At the far left and far right direction extremes, both curves approach the bottom of
      the normalized-response axis.
- [ ] The left and right halves of each curve are approximately mirror-symmetric around the
      central preferred direction.
- [ ] The generated model panel does not include empirical error bars; model sample markers
      on the tuning curves are acceptable.
- [ ] The legend or direct labels identify the attend-fixation condition separately from the
      attend-opposite-hemifield-stimulus condition.

## Cross-panel and protocol checks

- [ ] The generated model output focuses on the panel C simulation rather than trying to
      digitize or pointwise match panel B empirical data.
- [ ] The panel C model result shows sharpening, not simple multiplicative scaling: the two
      curves differ in width, not only in vertical amplitude.
- [ ] The panel C model result is not a contrast-response plot: the x-axis represents motion
      direction, not stimulus contrast.
- [ ] The panel C model result has no dashed percent-modulation curve; Figure 6C is a
      tuning-curve comparison, not a contrast-response modulation panel.
