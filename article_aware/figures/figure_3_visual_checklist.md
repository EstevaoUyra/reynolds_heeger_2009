# Figure 3 - Visual Checklist

Each item is a binary pass/fail visual claim for the generated model figure. No paper access
is required. Figure 3 in the paper contains empirical reference panels, but those empirical
data are not generated by the implementation and should not be used as pass/fail targets.

---

## Scope and coordinate convention

- **Reproduction target:** the generated figure should show the model simulations for the
  Figure 3C and Figure 3F protocols.
- **Non-target empirical panels:** paper panels B and E are empirical reference data. They
  should be ignored for detailed reproduction checking unless a generated figure explicitly
  includes them only as labeled context. Do not require matching empirical data points,
  error bars, firing-rate values, or source labels.
- **3C condition:** Reynolds et al. 2000-style model simulation with stimulus size 5,
  attention field size 30, peak gain 2, and Figure 3 baseline handling.
- **3F condition:** Williford and Maunsell 2006-style model simulation with stimulus size
  7, attention field size 7, peak gain 2, and Figure 3 baseline handling.
- **CRF x-axis:** log contrast, increasing left to right.
- **CRF response axis:** normalized model response, with larger values higher on the plot.
- **Percent-modulation axis:** attentional modulation in percent; larger values are higher
  on the dashed modulation curve.
- **Curve encoding:** attended/RF condition and unattended/away condition must be visually
  distinguishable as two solid response curves. Percent modulation must be a separate
  dashed curve or otherwise clearly distinct from the response curves.
- **Absolute difference:** attended response minus unattended response at the same contrast.
  If plotted as a separate panel, it should use the same log-contrast ordering as the CRF.

---

## Overall generated figure structure

- [ ] The generated figure includes a clearly labeled model output for the 3C condition.
- [ ] The generated figure includes a clearly labeled model output for the 3F condition.
- [ ] The generated figure does not require reproduction of empirical data points or error
      bars from paper panels B and E.
- [ ] The 3C and 3F model outputs use the same contrast-axis direction: low contrast on the
      left and high contrast on the right.
- [ ] The attended and unattended response curves are distinguishable in every CRF panel.
- [ ] The percent-modulation curve is visually distinguishable from the two response curves
      in every CRF panel that overlays modulation.
- [ ] If absolute-difference panels are included, there is one for 3C and one for 3F.
- [ ] If task schematics are included, they are clearly secondary context rather than the
      primary model-output comparison target.

---

## Optional schematic/context checks

Use these only if the generated figure includes schematic panels for the two protocols.
Skip this section if the generated figure contains only model-output plots.

- [ ] The 3C schematic shows a smaller stimulus than the 3F schematic.
- [ ] The 3C schematic shows an attention field that is much larger than its stimulus.
- [ ] The 3F schematic shows an attention field comparable in size to its larger stimulus.
- [ ] Both schematics show the attended/RF stimulus and away/unattended condition in a way
      that is visually consistent with the curve labels.

---

## 3C model CRF - Reynolds et al. 2000-style condition

- [ ] The 3C CRF panel contains two solid, monotonically increasing response curves.
- [ ] The attended response curve is never below the unattended response curve.
- [ ] Both response curves start above zero or visibly above the response-axis baseline at
      the lowest contrast, reflecting Figure 3 baseline activity.
- [ ] The attended response curve is left-shifted relative to the unattended response curve:
      its steep rising portion begins at a lower contrast.
- [ ] The attended curve reaches its inflection point or half-maximum at a lower contrast
      than the unattended curve.
- [ ] Both response curves saturate toward the right side of the plot.
- [ ] The attended and unattended response curves have little vertical separation at the
      highest contrasts compared with their separation on the rising portion.
- [ ] The percent-modulation curve is highest at the low end or low-to-intermediate part
      of the visible contrast range.
- [ ] The percent-modulation curve decreases toward high contrast.
- [ ] The percent-modulation curve is near zero or much smaller at the highest contrast
      than at its low-contrast maximum.

---

## 3C absolute-difference output

Use this section if the generated figure includes an explicit 3C attended-minus-unattended
panel. If no explicit absolute-difference panel is generated, evaluate the same relationship
from the vertical separation of the two solid CRF curves.

- [ ] The 3C absolute-difference curve is nonnegative across the full contrast range.
- [ ] The 3C absolute-difference curve is not visually largest at the leftmost contrast.
- [ ] The 3C absolute-difference curve is smaller at the high-contrast endpoint than the
      high-contrast endpoint of the 3F absolute-difference curve.

---

## 3F model CRF - Williford and Maunsell 2006-style condition

- [ ] The 3F CRF panel contains two solid, monotonically increasing response curves.
- [ ] The attended response curve is never below the unattended response curve.
- [ ] Both response curves start above zero or visibly above the response-axis baseline at
      the lowest contrast, reflecting Figure 3 baseline activity.
- [ ] The attended curve is primarily elevated above the unattended curve, rather than
      showing the strong leftward shift seen in 3C.
- [ ] The attended and unattended curves rise over approximately the same visible
      log-contrast range.
- [ ] Both response curves saturate toward the right side of the plot.
- [ ] The attended response curve saturates at a higher level than the unattended response
      curve.
- [ ] The attended and unattended curves remain visibly separated at the highest contrasts.
- [ ] The high-contrast response separation in 3F is larger than the high-contrast response
      separation in 3C.
- [ ] The percent-modulation curve is highest at the low-contrast end of the visible range.
- [ ] The percent-modulation curve decreases as contrast increases; it should not peak at
      high contrast.
- [ ] The percent-modulation curve remains visibly above zero at high contrast rather than
      collapsing all the way to the axis baseline.

---

## 3F absolute-difference output

Use this section if the generated figure includes an explicit 3F attended-minus-unattended
panel. If no explicit absolute-difference panel is generated, evaluate the same relationship
from the vertical separation of the two solid CRF curves.

- [ ] The 3F absolute-difference curve is nonnegative across the full contrast range.
- [ ] The 3F absolute-difference curve reaches its largest value in the high-contrast half
      of the x-axis.
- [ ] The 3F absolute-difference curve is clearly larger at high contrast than at the
      leftmost low contrast.
- [ ] The high-contrast endpoint of the 3F absolute-difference curve is higher than the
      high-contrast endpoint of the 3C absolute-difference curve.

---

## Cross-condition checks

- [ ] 3C shows the stronger leftward shift of the attended response curve.
- [ ] 3F shows the stronger high-contrast vertical separation between attended and
      unattended response curves.
- [ ] In both 3C and 3F, percent modulation is largest at lower contrasts.
- [ ] In 3F, the largest absolute difference occurs at a higher contrast than the largest
      percent modulation.
- [ ] The generated figure makes the distinction between percent modulation and absolute
      attended-minus-unattended difference visually clear.
- [ ] The generated figure can pass without reproducing paper panels B and E, because those
      panels are empirical reference data rather than model simulation outputs.
